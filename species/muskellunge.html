Species · Muskellunge

Muskellunge

Stocked by ODNR at Alum Creek. A fish of 10,000 casts, best in fall on big baits along weed edges, cove mouths and causeways.

Not a regular at Hoover: ODNR doesn't list it there. This page covers Alum Creek Lake.

Not a regular at Buckeye Lake: ODNR doesn't list it there. This page covers Alum Creek Lake.

Season Where Limits Gear Bait

Season

  • Spring: warming coves and the Big Run arm after the spawn, slower baits.
  • Summer: trolling the causeways, dam riprap and cove mouths in low light.
  • Fall: the best push of the year, October into November.
  • Winter: slow and rare; most anglers put the big rods away.

Time of day

Low light helps, but muskie bite in daylight too. In summer heat, fish early and late and keep fights short.

Where on Alum Creek

  • Causeway riprap and rocky points from public shore, long casts with bucktails or big spoons.
  • Public ramps, parks and shore only. Pins on the water maps.
  • Weed edges and cove mouths north of Cheshire Road; troll the causeways and dam riprap in summer.
  • Contours on the ODNR fishing map; check no wake zones before a night run.

Gear

  • Heavy rod, 65 to 80 lb braid, fluorocarbon or wire leader.
  • Long-nose pliers, jaw spreaders, bolt cutters for hooks and a big net.
  • Headlamp for unhooking after dark.

Bait & presentation

  • Bucktails, big spoons, glide baits and crankbaits (plugs).
  • Trolling crankbaits along causeway rock and cove mouths in summer.
  • Figure eight at the boat on every cast.

Notes

Most Ohio muskie anglers release their fish. Keep it in the water, unhook fast and support the belly for photos.

Water & maps

Contours, pool correction, public ramps.
