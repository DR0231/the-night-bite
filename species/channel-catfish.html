Species · Channel catfish

Channel catfish

Hoover’s workhorse night fish — reliable on cut bait and crawlers from bank or 10 hp boat.

Season Where Limits Gear Bait

Season

  • Ice-out / early spring: slow but starting — mud-line banks warm first.
  • Spring: pre-spawn and spawn push fish shallow; night bank bites pick up.
  • Summer: classic night fishery — after dark is often the best window.
  • Fall: feed-up before cool-down; cut bait still works.
  • Winter: deep and slow; daytime can outfish night when water is cold.

Time of day

Night is the strength for channels here — lantern hours after the boat traffic dies. Dawn/dusk also produce, especially in spring and fall.

Where on Hoover

  • Public flats near Walnut Road lower ramp (flat vs nearby drop).
  • Oxbow / upper arms for quieter bank nights.
  • Channel edges and cuts (Smothers / causeway pinch points) when you’re in a boat — stay legal, 10 hp.
  • Public shoreline only — no private docks.

Gear

Sized for bank reality and ≤10 hp boats — Hoover is a drinking-water reservoir.

  • Medium–heavy spinning or baitcasting, 12–20 lb line.
  • Bank: rod holders, headlamp (red if you can), net.
  • Boat (≤10 hp): same rods; keep it simple — no need for heavy tournament gear.
  • Circle or kahle hooks sized to bait; weight enough to hold bottom in light current.

Bait & presentation

  • Cut shad / cut bait, nightcrawlers, chicken liver, prepared stink baits.
  • Bank: still-fish on the bottom near a break or flat.
  • Boat: drift or anchor on channel edges / flats after dark.

Notes

Strong channel population per ODNR Hoover Fishing Area notes. Night is still this site’s focus.
