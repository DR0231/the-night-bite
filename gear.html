Hoover gear guide

Phone-first kits for Hoover Reservoir — cats, crappie, bass, saugeye. Bank vs boat cards, short buy lists, and packing checklists. Notebook voice.

Disclosure: No paid affiliate IDs on this page yet. If affiliate or sponsored links are added later, they will be labeled. Buy what fits your budget; confirm regs and safety gear yourself.

Overview Before you go Cats Crappie Bass Saugeye Packing

Overview

Hoover Reservoir (Westerville / Delaware–Franklin) is one of Ohio’s best multi-species lakes, especially for catfish. About ~2,800 acres, ~45 miles of shoreline, depths to ~60 ft near the dam.

10 HP outboard limit — idle-speed drinking-water reservoir. Lower pressure than many big lakes; kayaks and small boats work well. Night helps channel and flathead (blues too); it is not best for every species.

Before you go

License required ages 16+. Confirm 2026–27 rules on ODNR / HuntFish OH before you keep fish.

Species Reminder (VERIFY ONLY)
Crappie Hoover long had 9″ min; daily bag often 20 or 30 depending on site-specific table — verify
Bass LM/SM Typically 5/day, 12″ min
Walleye / saugeye / sauger 6 combined; size often 14–15″ — verify
Cats No limit on smaller fish; statewide trophy usually 1 channel ≥28″, 1 blue ≥35″, 1 flathead ≥35″. Older Hoover-specific slot language (18–28″ channel/blue) appeared in past booklets — check current pamphlet

Local intel

Old Dutchman Bait & Tackle, 904 S Sunbury Rd, Westerville — cut shad, shrimp, liver, crawlers, goldfish, live bluegill, maps, floating-rig parts.

North end (Oxbow / Twin Bridges / above Sunbury Rd) often cited for bigger blues.

  • Boat: fish finder / FFS helps crappie, saugeye, and cats

1. Catfish — Channel, Blue, Flathead

Headline Hoover fishery. Blues commonly run into the 30–50+ lb class. Night is strong for channel and flathead (blues too); daytime boat work still scores.

Optimal kits

Everyday cats

  • Med–heavy spinning or baitcast
  • 12–20 lb mono / fluoro or 20–30 lb braid + fluoro leader
  • Circle hooks 2/0–5/0 · egg / no-roll sinkers ½–3 oz
  • Rod holders (2–3) · headlamp · net · pliers
  • Measuring board (28″ / 35″ trophy lines)

Trophy blues / flatheads

  • Heavy baitcast or stout spinning
  • 40–65 lb braid · 40–60 lb mono/fluoro leader
  • Circle hooks 5/0–8/0 · heavier sinkers / 3-way
  • Large net · strong rod holders · cooler + ice
  • Circle-hook tip: wait for steady pull, then reel — don’t “set” hard

Rigs (buy list)

RigPieces
Slip-sinker / Carolina Egg sinker → bead → swivel → 18–36″ leader → circle hook
Floating / hover Same as slip + foam float or floating hook to lift bait off silt
3-way 3-way swivel · dropper to sinker · longer arm to bait (channel edges)
Balloon mid-depth (boat) Balloon or float set bait mid-column while slow-trolling cut bait
Bank night Rod holders · slip or floating · clicker / drag set · headlamp

Baits

  • Cut shad / skipjack · shrimp · chicken liver · nightcrawlers
  • Live bluegill / goldfish where legal — verify bait rules
  • Flatheads lean live; blues love fresh cut; channels eat almost anything smelly

Bank vs boat

Bank

  • Public shoreline near Oxbow, Redbank, Walnut flats
  • Night: multiple rods in holders, quiet lights
  • Watch private docks — public only

Boat / kayak

  • Channel edges, dam basin, north-end deeper holes
  • 3-way or balloon troll for blues
  • ≤10 HP · PFD · fish finder helps

2. Crappie

9″ minimum long noted for Hoover — verify HuntFish OH / ODNR 2026–27.

Optimal kit

  • Light spinning 4–8 lb mono or light braid + fluoro leader
  • 1/16–1/8 oz jigs · slip floats · small hooks
  • Ruler for 9″ · small net · minnow bucket if using live bait legally

Rigs

  • Slip-float: bead · stop · float · small shot · jig or minnow — depth tune under brush / docks (public)
  • Vertical jig: boat over brush / timber · slow lift-drop
  • Cast-retrieve: small curly-tail or tube along points and riprap

Baits / lures

  • Live minnows · small plastics (chartreuse, white, natural)
  • Tungsten or lead jigs matched to wind

Bank vs boat

Bank

  • Riprap, bridges, public docks / shore brush
  • Slip-float covers depth without a graph

Boat

  • Brush piles, creek arms, suspended schools
  • FFS / sonar speeds finding timber

3. Bass

Largemouth primary on Hoover; smallmouth show in rockier / clearer stretches. Stick worms are a Hoover staple.

Optimal kit

  • Medium spinning or baitcast · 8–14 lb line (or 10–20 lb braid + leader)
  • Soft plastics · spinnerbait / chatter · topwater for low light
  • Terminal kit · pliers · 12″ measure (black bass min — verify)

Rigs / presentations

  • Texas-rig stick worm / creature — weightless or light bullet
  • Wacky stick worm — docks, grass edges, calm pockets
  • Jig + trailer — wood, rock, deeper banks
  • Search: spinnerbait, squarebill, chatterbait
  • Dropshot / Ned — clear water / pressured fish

Colors

Green pumpkin, watermelon, black/blue, white/chartreuse for stained water; natural when clear.

Bank vs boat

Bank

  • Points, riprap, laydowns on public shore
  • Weightless stick worm covers water quietly

Boat

  • Creek channel swings, brush, dam riprap
  • Cover water with search baits, then slow down

4. Saugeye / Walleye

Hoover gets saugeye / walleye attention via stocking programs over the years — check current ODNR notes. Daily 6 combined walleye/sauger/saugeye is the usual reminder; size often 14–15″ — verify.

Optimal kit

  • Medium spinning · 6–10 lb fluoro or braid + leader
  • Jigheads 1/8–3/8 oz · paddle-tails / twisters · blades / spoons
  • Headlamp for low-light unhooking · long-nose pliers

Rigs / methods

  • Jig + minnow or soft plastic — slow hops on bottom
  • Blade / spoon — vertical or cast when fish are up
  • Slow troll crankbaits or harness (boat, ≤10 HP)
  • Bank dam / riprap — cast after dark or low light

Baits

  • Live minnows · soft plastics (white, chartreuse, natural perch)
  • Thin crankbaits that dig without snagging constantly

Bank vs boat

Bank

  • Dam / lower riprap when open and legal
  • Points and current edges after dark

Boat

  • Channel edges, flats adjacent to deep water
  • Slow troll or vertical when marked on sonar

Quick packing lists

Bank day — mixed

  • License · polarized glasses · sunscreen / bug spray
  • Medium spinning + light spinning (or one medium + terminal swap)
  • Plastics / jigs · one cat rod if targeting cats
  • Pliers · clippers · measure · small net
  • Water · snacks · trash bag

Boat / kayak

  • All bank items + wearable PFD · throwable if required
  • Anchor · lights · dry bag · whistle
  • Fish finder if you have one · spare kill-switch lanyard
  • Cooler + ice · measuring board · large net
  • Stay ≤10 HP · pack out everything

Practical Hoover notes

  • Night is better for channel and flathead (blues too) — not a rule for crappie/bass every trip.
  • Wind piles bait on windward banks and points.
  • North end often cited for trophy blues (Oxbow / Twin Bridges / above Sunbury Rd).
  • Catch bait on an ultralight when legal and practical.
  • 10 HP limit → a kayak covers a lot of lake without racing elsewhere.
