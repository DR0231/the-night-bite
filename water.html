How to read this
  • Pool is lake surface elevation (ft NGVD29) — not the depth of any hole or channel.
  • The ODNR contour map is drawn at 894 ft. Contours are off by about (894 − pool).
  • Temp is gauge water ~2 ft down — not air temp, and not every creek arm. Lead shows °F; °C lives here.
  • USGS values are provisional (params 62614 / 00010). USGS-03228400.
  • Hoover is a 10 HP / idle-speed drinking-water reservoir — keep wakes down and pack out trash.

Structure key

First-person notebook — how I read the lake for cats and low-light predators.

  • Old Big Walnut channel still runs under the lake. I treat it as the buried highway — where bait and current sense often line up after dark.
  • Dam / main basin is the deepest water on the 894 map (often 50–60+ ft on that chart). Big water, boat traffic by day, more room to dig at night.
  • Causeway / Smothers cuts are the pinch points — bridges, cuts, and current funnels where I listen for bait and watch the graph harder.
  • Walnut Road lower ramp: nearby flat vs drop. I use the flats for bank work and the nearby break when I’m in a boat and want a quick edge.
  • Oxbow / upper arms: shallower, more bank and night work. Quieter when the middle lake is busy; watch for silted edges.
  • 10 hp / idle-speed rules apply. Hoover is a Columbus drinking-water reservoir — keep it clean, keep wakes down, follow posted limits.
